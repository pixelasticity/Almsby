# D-003 — Maker voice and authorship remain primary

## Context

The primary UX persona is a craft-focused maker who cares about preserving the meaning and individuality of their work.

AI can make writing and organization easier, but polished generated copy can also make products sound generic or disconnect the maker from their own story.

## Decision

Almsby's AI should **amplify the maker's voice rather than replace it**.

AI may:

- Ask thoughtful questions
- Help uncover useful details
- Organize information
- Suggest structure
- Improve clarity
- Offer interpretations
- Present suggestions for approval

AI should not silently:

- Invent personal history
- Manufacture emotional meaning
- Replace the maker's voice
- Turn a product into generic marketing copy
- Present its own interpretation as the maker's intention

## Alternatives considered

### Fully autonomous story generation

Rejected because it risks loss of authorship and authenticity.

### Traditional form-only story creation

Rejected because it places too much of the organization and writing burden on the maker.

### AI as an editor only

Too restrictive because AI can provide greater value by helping the maker discover, organize, and articulate knowledge before editing.

## Why

The product should make the maker's invisible craftsmanship and intent more visible without taking ownership of it.

The maker should remain the author.

## Consequences

- AI story workflows should be collaborative.
- Suggestions should be distinguishable from user-authored content where that distinction matters.
- The UX should preserve user control over generated or transformed content.
- Generic marketing language should be treated as a quality failure.
- Design and critique should evaluate whether the maker's voice survives the workflow.

## Revisit when

Reconsider if research demonstrates a different user need or if users explicitly prefer a materially different authorship model.
