# Product Constitution

## What we're building

A Saas platform that makes GS1-compliant 2D barcodes and Digital Product Passports dramatically easier to create and manage.

Our ambition is to make the complexity of product identity infrastructure invisible to the people who need to use it.

We believe every product has a story, and a product's digital identity can be more than a compliance requirement: it can become a meaningful connection between the product, its maker, and the oerson who owns it.

## Our promise

**Every product has a story. Tell that story.**

## What we believe

### 1. Complexity belongs in the product, not the user's head.

Users should not need to become experts in GS1, Digital Link, DPPs, identifiers, or barcode technology to use our platform correctly.

### 2. Make the next step obvious.

The user should rarely have to figure out what to do next.

The product should explain, guide, validate, and recommend.

### 3. Automate the work. Expose the decisions.

When the system can safely do work on the user's behalf, it should.

When a consequential decision requires user judgement, the system should:

**Detect → Explain → Recommend → Approve**

We should avoid silently making consequential changes.

### 4. Prefer understandable language over technical terminology.

Technical concepts should exist underneath the experience.

Expose technical details when they are useful, necessary, or requested—not merely because they exist.

### 5. Transparency creates trust.

The user should understand:

* What the system is doing
* Why it is asking for something
* What it found
* What it recommends
* What it changed
* What the user is approving
* What the user is paying for

### 6. Reliability beats cleverness.

A barcode and digital product identity are infrastructure.

If something fails in the physical world, the consequences can be serious.

We should favor predictable, verifiable behavior over clever but fragile experiences.

### 7. Predictability builds trust.

Users should not be surprised by:

* pricing,
* changes to their data,
* compliance requirements,
* system behavior,
* or what happens to their products.

### 8. Compliance is the entry point, not necessarily the destination.

Some users come because they have to become compliant.

Others will discover that the same infrastructure can support storytelling, customer relationships, product care, provenance, community, and other experiences.

We should solve the immediate problem exceptionally well before asking users to care about the broader vision.

### 9. The physical product is a relationship opportunity.

A digital identity attached to a physical product can connect a brand or maker with the person who owns the product—even when the original transaction happened through another channel.

We want that connection to feel useful and meaningful, not like an advertisement pasted onto the product.

### 10. Every product has a story.

The maker may know why a product exists deeply but struggle to communicate that story.

We should help them tell it.

A story page should help a customer understand not merely **what** a product is, but **why it exists**.

## What we don't want

We do not want to become:

* A generic QR-code generator
* A compliance dashboard that requires an expert to operate
* A maze of GS1 terminology
* A tool that makes users fill in information the system could discover
* A black box that silently changes important product data
* An advertising platform disguised as a product experience
* Enterprise software that sacrifices usability for configurability
* A beautiful storytelling tool that neglects compliance and reliability


## The emotional goal

For the maker:

> “My customers finally understand my work.”

For the compliance-driven customer:

> “I can't believe it was so easy.”

For the enterprise:

> “This gives us a direct relationship with the people who own our products.”

### The simplest test

When evaluating a product decision, ask:

>Does this make the user's life easier while preserving their understanding and control?

If not, reconsider it.
